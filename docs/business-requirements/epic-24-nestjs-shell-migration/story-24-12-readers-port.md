# Story 24.12: Readers Port

## Description

**As a** backend engineer,
**I want to** port the `readers` module — the largest module in the codebase (**11 routes**; `SegmenterService`/`PassageGenerationService`/`ReadersAudioService` + the DB-backed **5/day generation rate-limit**) — from Express (`ReadersController.ts`/`readersRoutes.ts` + the root-instantiated service singletons in `app/container.ts`) to the NestJS 11 shell under `modules/readers/nest/`, moving the three root-instantiated services into Nest module providers, resolving `ReadersAudioService`'s audio dependency through the ported `AudioModule` (Nest-injected `AudioService` + `AUDIO_PASSAGE_PATHS` — **no direct `modules/audio` function import in Nest land**), and reproducing the DB-backed 5/day generation rate-limit,
**So that** the largest reader surface becomes contract-identical on the Nest shell with the **post-calibration** auth semantics — the three public-ish reads (`GET /v1/readers/passages`, `GET /v1/readers/passages/:id`, `POST /v1/readers/passages/:id/audio`) mounted with the calibrated `OptionalAuthGuard` (F5: a guest is **never 401'd**; passage-audio is **cache-first free for guests**) and the user-scoped / cost-bearing routes (`generate`, `sessions/*`, `bookmarks/*`) mounted with `RequireAuthGuard` (guest → 401 `AUTH_REQUIRED`) — while the absorbed epic-25 **F5 passage-audio** surface and the DB-backed 5/day generation limit land exactly per the Express wire contract, all proven by a dedicated parity harness (real Express vs real Nest) that keeps the migration additive with the Express production path untouched until the 24-15 cutover.

## Business Value

The readers port is the **largest and most dependency-carrying port** of the migration: 11 routes spanning three controller surfaces (passage list/detail/audio + generation, per-passage reading sessions, and per-user bookmarks) wired to **three root-instantiated services** (`SegmenterService`/`PassageGenerationService`/`ReadersAudioService`) that previously lived in `app/container.ts` rather than the module's own factory. It is the first port that moves service ownership INTO the Nest module (a pattern the remaining ports — most immediately **24-13 quiz+progression** — will rely on) and the first **cross-module audio consumer**: `ReadersAudioService` consumes the `AudioService` facade and the passage path helpers (`passageHashFor`/`passagePath`) **from the ported `AudioModule` via Nest DI** (`AudioService` + the additive `AUDIO_PASSAGE_PATHS` provider, established in 24-10), replacing the Express wiring's direct `modules/audio/index.js` function import — no direct cross-module barrel import in Nest land.

The story also lands the **calibrated epic-25 F5 passage-audio surface**: the audio route (`POST /v1/readers/passages/:id/audio`) is mounted with the calibrated `OptionalAuthGuard` — a guest (no/bad token) is **never 401'd** and gets **cache-first free reads** (a GCS cache HIT re-signs the short-lived URL with `source: "gcs"` and no billable TTS generation; GCS cold-cache is the cost protector). The **DB-backed 5/day generation limit** is reproduced exactly as the Express wire contract: `ReadersService.checkRateLimits` → `ReadersRepository.countUserGeneratedToday` (UTC-midnight reset) with the 5-passage storage cap, mapped to 429 `RATE_LIMIT` on the shell. A dedicated parity harness (`readers-parity.test.ts`, **28 tests**) boots the real Express app + real Nest `AppModule` in-process — proving 2xx deep-equal (with non-deterministic-field normalization), 4xx/5xx envelope parity, guest 401s, the 5/day 429 (with **no Gemini call** on the blocked request), sessions/bookmarks upserts, and mocked-GCS audio cache-hit/miss — while the Express production surface stays untouched (dual-mode) until 24-15.

## Business Rules

1. **Routes are verbatim from `ROUTE_PATTERNS`** — the 11 readers paths are the `ROUTE_PATTERNS.readers*` entries (`readersPassages`, `readersPassageById`, `readersGenerate`, `readersPassageAudioById`, `readersSessionByPassageId`, `readersSessionCompleteByPassageId`, `readersBookmarks`, `readersBookmarkByPassageId`), `/api` prefix applied by the shell; the Nest controller mirrors `api/ReadersController.ts` 1:1, reusing the framework-agnostic services unchanged (no re-write of segmentation/passage-generation/audio internals).
2. **Service-ownership move** — `SegmenterService`/`PassageGenerationService`/`ReadersAudioService` move from root-instantiation in `app/container.ts` into `ReadersModule` `useFactory` providers (constructor-injected with `CacheService`/`GeminiService`/`AudioService`+`AUDIO_PASSAGE_PATHS`); `ReadersRepository` + `ReadersService` are provided the same way (`ReadersService` with the same five deps `createReadersModule(deps)` passes). The Express surface keeps the **same classes** (dual-mode) — only the wiring differs.
3. **Calibrated auth per route** — the three public-ish reads (`passages`, `passages/:id`, `passages/:id/audio`) mount the **calibrated `OptionalAuthGuard`** (24-5): guest (no/bad token) → `req.userId` undefined, never 401; a guest lists shared passages and reads passage audio **free** (cache-first). The user-scoped / cost-bearing routes (`generate`, `sessions/*`, `bookmarks/*`) mount **`RequireAuthGuard`**: guest → 401 `AUTH_REQUIRED` before the controller; the `if (!userId)` 401 `AUTH_ERROR` checks on those routes are defense-in-depth mirroring the Express controller structure (unreachable under the guard).
4. **F5 passage-audio cache-first-free-for-guests** — the audio route is `@UseGuards(OptionalAuthGuard)`; a guest (or user) hits the same two-tier fallback (GCS exists-check → re-sign `source: "gcs"`; miss → synthesize+upload `source: "ondemand"`); GCS cold-cache is the cost protector (a cache HIT never bills Google TTS). Per-sentence failures are reported as `source: "failed"` — never a 5xx for audio infra failures.
5. **DB-backed 5/day generation limit** — NOT an `express-rate-limit` window: `ReadersService.checkRateLimits` counts `ReadersRepository.countUserGeneratedToday` (UTC midnight) against `MAX_DAILY_GENERATIONS = 5` and `countUserGenerated` against the `MAX_USER_PASSAGES = 5` storage cap; `READERS_DAILY_GENERATION_LIMIT = 5` declared in `rate-limit.config.ts`; `RateLimitExceededError` → 429 `RATE_LIMIT` envelope. The per-route `express-rate-limit` GET limiters (60/min user, 20/min guest) apply only to the two passage GET routes (`rateLimitReadersByAuth` mounted path-scoped in `configure-app.ts`), mirroring `readersRoutes.ts`; the audio POST / generate / sessions / bookmarks routes carry no `express-rate-limit`, exactly like Express.
6. **`@HttpCode` status parity** — Nest POST defaults (201) overridden where Express sends a different status: audio POST `@HttpCode(200)`, generate `@HttpCode(201)` (matches Nest default but pinned for explicitness), complete `@HttpCode(200)`, addBookmark `@HttpCode(201)`, bookmark DELETE `@HttpCode(204)` + empty body (`res.status(204).send()`).
7. **Additive-only** — the Express readers wiring (`container.ts`, `api/ReadersController.ts`, `api/readersRoutes.ts`, the root-instantiated service singletons) is untouched; the Nest surface coexists until the module cutover (24-15). No `packages/shared-constants` / `packages/shared-types` / FE change. `check:module-boundaries` stays green.
